## Microspine arrays: the Stanford line

The papers in which the compliant microspine array was introduced, analysed and scaled up.

- <a id="asbeck2005"></a>**Asbeck 2005.** Alan T. Asbeck, Sangbae Kim, Mark R. Cutkosky, William R. Provancher and Michele Lanzetta (2005). Scaling hard vertical surfaces with compliant microspine arrays. *Robotics: Science and Systems I (Proceedings of Robotics: Science and Systems 2005, Cambridge, MA)*. [doi:10.15607/rss.2005.i.026](https://doi.org/10.15607/rss.2005.i.026) · [open copy](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss01/p26.pdf)  
  Introduces arrays of microspines on compliant, multi-link toe suspensions that let a robot climb concrete, stucco, brick and masonry walls without suction or adhesives. Discusses how spine size should scale with surface roughness and how the suspension should distribute tensile and shear loads among many spines.

- <a id="kim2005"></a>**Kim 2005.** Sangbae Kim, Alan T. Asbeck, Mark R. Cutkosky and William R. Provancher (2005). SpinybotII: climbing hard walls with compliant microspines. *ICAR '05: Proceedings of the 12th International Conference on Advanced Robotics (Seattle, WA)*, pp. 601–606. [doi:10.1109/icar.2005.1507470](https://doi.org/10.1109/icar.2005.1507470) · [open copy](http://bdml.stanford.edu/twiki/pub/Rise/RisePublications/IEEE_ICARv11.pdf)  
  Presents SpinybotII, a climbing robot that scales flat, hard vertical surfaces (concrete, brick, stucco, masonry) using arrays of miniature spines that catch on surface asperities, carrying a payload equal to its own weight and clinging without consuming power. Covers spine/surface interaction analysis and compliant suspension design.

- <a id="asbeck2006"></a>**Asbeck 2006.** Alan T. Asbeck, Sangbae Kim, M. R. Cutkosky, William R. Provancher and Michele Lanzetta (2006). Scaling Hard Vertical Surfaces with Compliant Microspine Arrays. *The International Journal of Robotics Research* 25(12), pp. 1165–1179. [doi:10.1177/0278364906072511](https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364906072511) · [open copy](https://mech.utah.edu/files/2012/01/asbeck2006-IJRR-Scaling_Hard_Vertical_Surfaces_with_Compliant_Microspine_Arrays.pdf)  
  Journal version of the microspine approach: discusses spine scaling versus surface roughness, models the spine/surface interaction, and details the toe-suspension properties needed for each spine to find a usable asperity and for many spines to share load. Demonstrates the principles on SpinybotII and reports how toe parameters scale with robot mass, including use on the larger RiSE robot.

- <a id="asbeck2006video"></a>**Asbeck 2006 (video).** Alan T. Asbeck, Sangbae Kim, Arthur McClung, Aaron Parness and Mark R. Cutkosky (2006). Climbing Walls with Microspines. *IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA 2006), Orlando, FL — short paper accompanying a video submission*. [PDF from the authors](http://bdml.stanford.edu/twiki/pub/Rise/RisePublications/ICRA06_spines.pdf)  
  Short paper (with accompanying video) summarising the design principles of climbing with microspines on Spinybot and their adaptation to the larger, heavier RiSE platform, including how toe compliances should scale with robot mass, spine tip radius and number of toes.

- <a id="asbeck2010"></a>**Asbeck 2010.** Alan Thomas Asbeck (2010). Compliant directional suspensions for climbing with spines and adhesives. *Ph.D. thesis, Stanford University (submitted to the Department of Electrical Engineering)*. [Stanford Digital Repository](https://purl.stanford.edu/xt008ys4403)  
  Doctoral thesis on synthetic microspine arrays for robot and human climbing: surface properties suited to spines, a spine/surface interaction model, the suspension properties needed for conformation, load sharing, engagement and disengagement, measured performance of built suspensions, and extension of the same principles to directional dry adhesives and large patches.

- <a id="asbeck2012"></a>**Asbeck & Cutkosky 2012.** Alan T. Asbeck and Mark R. Cutkosky (2012). Designing Compliant Spine Mechanisms for Climbing. *Journal of Mechanisms and Robotics* 4(3), article 031007. [doi:10.1115/1.40066591](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.40066591)  
  Presents design principles for the compliant mechanisms that support and load spines on rough vertical surfaces, so that spine/surface constraints are satisfied and multiple spines share load without premature failure or overloading. The principles are demonstrated with a mechanism used for robotic and human climbing on brick, stucco and concrete.

- <a id="wang2016"></a>**Wang 2016.** Shiquan Wang, Hao Jiang and Mark R. Cutkosky (2016). A palm for a rock climbing robot based on dense arrays of micro-spines. *2016 IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS)*, pp. 52–59. [doi:10.1109/iros.2016.7759034](https://doi.org/10.1109/iros.2016.7759034)  
  Presents a palm with a dense array of micro-spines for the JPL RoboSimian human-scale climbing robot, built from a linearly-constrained spine mechanism analysed with adhesion and stiffness models. A 120 x 100 mm palm of 12 spine tiles with a pulley differential for load sharing is tested on nine surfaces.

- <a id="wang2017"></a>**Wang 2017.** Shiquan Wang, Hao Jiang and Mark R. Cutkosky (2017). Design and modeling of linearly-constrained compliant spines for human-scale locomotion on rocky surfaces. *The International Journal of Robotics Research* 36(9), pp. 985–999. [doi:10.1177/0278364917720019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364917720019)  
  Presents linearly-constrained spines for human-scale robots on steep rocky surfaces: low stiffness normal to the surface but high stiffness against lateral and bending loads, giving much higher spine density and less scraping than earlier arrays for small robots. Theory and experiments predict forces for single and opposed arrays, with applications including a gripper for a 'smart staff' and a palm for the RoboSimian quadruped.

- <a id="ruotolo2019"></a>**Ruotolo 2019.** Wilson Ruotolo, Frances S. Roig and Mark R. Cutkosky (2019). Load-Sharing in Soft and Spiny Paws for a Large Climbing Robot. *IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters* 4(2), pp. 1439–1446. [doi:10.1109/lra.2019.2897002](https://doi.org/10.1109/lra.2019.2897002) · [open copy](http://bdml.stanford.edu/pmwiki/uploads/Main/MobileManipulationPubs/SoftAndSpiny_RALFinal.pdf)  
  Presents an end-effector for a large climbing robot whose gripping surfaces are covered with sharp spines; soft flexure joints and a particle-jamming palm let it conform to varied shapes, and a differential distributes one actuator's force to the phalanges. Tests on instrumented surfaces confirm the predicted finger load sharing and palm performance.

- <a id="wang2019"></a>**Wang 2019.** Shiquan Wang, Hao Jiang, Tae Myung Huh, Danning Sun, Wilson Ruotolo, Matthew Miller, William R. T. Roderick, Hannah S. Stuart and Mark R. Cutkosky (2019). SpinyHand: Contact Load Sharing for a Human-Scale Climbing Robot. *Journal of Mechanisms and Robotics* 11(3), article 031009. [doi:10.1115/1.4043023](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4043023) · [open copy](http://bdml.stanford.edu/pmwiki/uploads/Main/MobileManipulationPubs/SpinyHandJMR-18-1464-min.pdf)  
  Presents a hand specialised for climbing unstructured rocky surfaces: articulated fingers achieve grasps used by human climbers, gripping surfaces carry dense spine arrays, and a load-sharing transmission divides shear force among spine tiles on each phalanx. The kinematics and load sharing allow the achievable wrench space to be computed rapidly and compared with bench-top tests.

## Models of spine–surface contact

- <a id="jiang2018"></a>**Jiang 2018.** Hao Jiang, Shiquan Wang and Mark R. Cutkosky (2018). Stochastic models of compliant spine arrays for rough surface grasping. *The International Journal of Robotics Research* 37(7), pp. 669–687. [doi:10.1177/0278364918778350](https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364918778350)  
  Models arrays of compliantly supported spines attaching to rough surfaces: surfaces are characterised by asperity distributions, giving stochastic models of spine force capability over a range of loading directions for unidirectional arrays and for opposed pairs that resist normal pull-off. Experiments on several surfaces confirm the predictions and show the role of preloading strategy for opposed arrays.

- <a id="iacoponi2020"></a>**Iacoponi 2020.** Saverio Iacoponi, Marcello Calisti and Cecilia Laschi (2020). Simulation and Analysis of Microspines Interlocking Behavior on Rocky Surfaces: An In-Depth Study of the Isolated Spine. *Journal of Mechanisms and Robotics* 12(6), article 061016. [doi:10.1115/1.4047725](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4047725) · [open copy](https://figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Simulation_and_Analysis_of_Microspines_Interlocking_Behavior_on_Rocky_Surfaces_An_In-Depth_Study_of_the_Isolated_Spine/24392731)  
  Proposes a model for the anchoring resistance of a single microspine on a local asperity and applies it in a simulation campaign that relates spine performance to macroscopic variables of the spine, the substrate and its usage, followed by experimental comparison tests.

## Fabrication

- <a id="cham2002"></a>**Cham 2002.** Jorge G. Cham, Sean A. Bailey, Jonathan E. Clark, Robert J. Full and Mark R. Cutkosky (2002). Fast and Robust: Hexapedal Robots via Shape Deposition Manufacturing. *The International Journal of Robotics Research* 21(10-11), pp. 869–882. [doi:10.1177/0278364902021010837](https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364902021010837)  
  Argues that biomimetic robot design should be combined with structures built the way biological ones are, with embedded actuators and sensors and spatially varied materials, enabled by Shape Deposition Manufacturing. Presents a family of fast, robust hexapedal robots made this way, with their design, fabrication and locomotion experiments.

- <a id="nadan2022"></a>**Nadan 2022.** Paul Nadan, Dinesh K. Patel, Catherine Pavlov, Spencer Backus and Aaron M. Johnson (2022). Microspine Design for Additive Manufacturing. *2022 IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS)*, pp. 5640–5647. [doi:10.1109/iros47612.2022.9981634](https://doi.org/10.1109/iros47612.2022.9981634)  
  Applies Hybrid Deposition Manufacturing to microspine fabrication and proposes a 3D-printed microspine suspension made by fused deposition alone from a single flexible material with an embedded fishhook, guided by a stiffness model. Tensile tests compare the printed designs with a standard Shape Deposition Manufacturing microspine.

## Legged and limbed climbers

- <a id="spenko2008"></a>**Spenko 2008.** M. J. Spenko, G. C. Haynes, J. A. Saunders, M. R. Cutkosky, A. A. Rizzi, R. J. Full and D. E. Koditschek (2008). Biologically inspired climbing with a hexapedal robot. *Journal of Field Robotics* 25(4-5), pp. 223–242. [doi:10.1002/rob.20238](https://doi.org/10.1002/rob.20238) · [open copy](http://bdml.stanford.edu/twiki/pub/Rise/RisePublications/rise-jfr-2007-3.pdf)  
  Systems-level description of the hexapedal RiSE robot, which locomotes on the ground and climbs vertical building surfaces such as brick, stucco and crushed stone without suction, magnets or adhesives. Covers body morphology, hierarchical compliance in legs and feet, and the sensing and control that make climbing reliable.

- <a id="wile2008"></a>**Wile 2008.** G. D. Wile, K. A. Daltorio, E. D. Diller, L. R. Palmer, S. N. Gorb, R. E. Ritzmann and R. D. Quinn (2008). Screenbot: Walking inverted using distributed inward gripping. *2008 IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS)*, pp. 1513–1518. [doi:10.1109/iros.2008.4651045](https://doi.org/10.1109/iros.2008.4651045)  
  Presents Screenbot, a small robot that scales wire-mesh substrates using spines, walking with an alternating tripod gait driven by a single motor while opposing legs maintain tension. The resulting distributed inward gripping lets it climb vertically, walk inverted on a screen ceiling and cling passively.

- <a id="haynes2009"></a>**Haynes 2009.** G. C. Haynes, A. Khripin, G. Lynch, J. Amory, A. Saunders, A. A. Rizzi and D. E. Koditschek (2009). Rapid pole climbing with a quadrupedal robot. *2009 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA)*, pp. 2767–2772. [doi:10.1109/robot.2009.5152830](https://doi.org/10.1109/robot.2009.5152830) · [open copy](https://repository.upenn.edu/handle/20.500.14332/33690)  
  Describes the RiSE V3 legged robot, designed for general locomotion but specialised for dynamical high-speed climbing of convex cylindrical structures such as telephone poles, with novel leg linkages and a non-backdrivable, energy-dense transmission. Documents a gait that rapidly ascends a wooden pole while passively maintaining yaw, pitch and roll stability.

- <a id="birkmeyer2011"></a>**Birkmeyer 2011.** P. Birkmeyer, A. G. Gillies and R. S. Fearing (2011). CLASH: Climbing vertical loose cloth. *2011 IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS)*, pp. 5087–5093. [doi:10.1109/iros.2011.6094905](https://doi.org/10.1109/iros.2011.6094905)  
  Presents CLASH, a small hexapedal robot with a single actuator and passive foot mechanisms that engage and disengage spines smoothly on penetrable surfaces, described as the first robot to climb loose vertical cloth.

- <a id="lynch2012"></a>**Lynch 2012.** Goran A. Lynch, Jonathan E. Clark, Pei-Chun Lin and Daniel E. Koditschek (2012). A bioinspired dynamical vertical climbing robot. *The International Journal of Robotics Research* 31(8), pp. 974–996. [doi:10.1177/0278364912442096](https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364912442096)  
  Describes the inspiration, design, analysis and implementation of a dynamical vertical climbing robot based on a pendulous climbing template proposed by biologists, studied numerically at a scale suited to conventional actuation. Analysis addresses the stability concerns raised by added passive compliance, and prototypes are compared with the models.

- <a id="xu2012"></a>**Xu 2012.** Fengyu Xu, Xingsong Wang and Guoping Jiang (2012). Design and Analysis of a Wall-Climbing Robot Based on a Mechanism Utilizing Hook-Like Claws. *International Journal of Advanced Robotic Systems* 9(6), article 261. [doi:10.5772/53895](https://doi.org/10.5772/53895) · open access  
  Proposes an attachment method for robots inspecting rough concrete walls based on hook-like claws, with a description of concrete surface roughness, two mechanical models of claw/micro-protuberance interaction and a design method for claw tips. An eight-foot wall-climbing robot is designed and tested on a man-made concrete wall.

- <a id="palmer2015"></a>**Palmer 2015.** Luther R., III Palmer, Eric Diller and Roger D. Quinn (2015). Toward Gravity-Independent Climbing Using a Biologically Inspired Distributed Inward Gripping Strategy. *IEEE/ASME Transactions on Mechatronics* 20(2), pp. 631–640. [doi:10.1109/tmech.2014.2315762](https://doi.org/10.1109/tmech.2014.2315762)  
  Presents distributed inward gripping (DIG), in which contralateral legs engage cockroach-inspired prehensile spines by pulling inward toward the body rather than downward against gravity, as a foot-attachment strategy for gravity-independent climbing. The test robot DIGbot climbs and turns on vertical and inverted mesh screen, with each foot searching locally for a foothold.

- <a id="shirai2020"></a>**Shirai 2020.** Yuki Shirai, Xuan Lin, Yusuke Tanaka, Ankur Mehta and Dennis Hong (2020). Risk-Aware Motion Planning for a Limbed Robot with Stochastic Gripping Forces Using Nonlinear Programming. *IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters* 5(4), pp. 4994–5001. [doi:10.1109/lra.2020.3001503](https://doi.org/10.1109/lra.2020.3001503) · [open copy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.02656)  
  Presents a motion planner with probabilistic guarantees for limbed robots whose gripping forces are stochastic, formulated as a chance-constrained nonlinear programme that plans pose and contact-force trajectories under a chosen risk bound, with gripping forces modelled by Gaussian process regression. Validated on a six-limbed robot climbing between two walls; the full text states that the robot uses spine grippers.

- <a id="uno2021"></a>**Uno 2021.** Kentaro Uno, Naomasa Takada, Taku Okawara, Keigo Haji, Arthur Candalot, Warley F. R. Ribeiro, Kenji Nagaoka and Kazuya Yoshida (2021). HubRobo: A Lightweight Multi-Limbed Climbing Robot for Exploration in Challenging Terrain. *2020 IEEE-RAS 20th International Conference on Humanoid Robots (Humanoids)*, pp. 209–215. [doi:10.1109/humanoids47582.2021.9555799](https://doi.org/10.1109/humanoids47582.2021.9555799)  
  Presents the design and sequential control strategy of a lightweight quadruped climbing robot with 3-DOF actuated limbs, a 3-DOF passive compliant spine gripper at each foot and an actuator to open and close each gripper. A teleoperated experiment on an indoor field simulating uneven Martian terrain demonstrates stable climbing.

- <a id="tanaka2022"></a>**Tanaka 2022.** Yusuke Tanaka, Yuki Shirai, Xuan Lin, Alexander Schperberg, Hayato Kato, Alexander Swerdlow, Naoya Kumagai and Dennis Hong (2022). SCALER: A Tough Versatile Quadruped Free-Climber Robot. *2022 IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS)*, pp. 5632–5639. [doi:10.1109/iros47612.2022.9981555](https://doi.org/10.1109/iros47612.2022.9981555) · [open copy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01180)  
  Introduces SCALER, a quadrupedal robot that climbs bouldering walls, overhangs and ceilings and trots on the ground, using a body linkage mechanism, a new climbing gait (SKATE) that increases payload, and the underactuated two-finger GOAT gripper.

- <a id="newdick2023"></a>**Newdick 2023.** Stephanie Newdick, Nitin Ongole, Tony G. Chen, Edward Schmerling, Mark R. Cutkosky and Marco Pavone (2023). Motion Planning for a Climbing Robot with Stochastic Grasps. *2023 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA)*, pp. 11838–11844. [doi:10.1109/icra48891.2023.10160218](https://doi.org/10.1109/icra48891.2023.10160218) · [open copy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10687)  
  Introduces motion planning for ReachBot that accounts for the stochastic nature of grasping with spines: a graph-traversal algorithm selects a discrete grasp sequence from available terrain features, complemented by a decoupled motion planner using sampling-based planning and sequential convex programming.

- <a id="nadan2024"></a>**Nadan 2024.** Paul Nadan, Spencer Backus and Aaron M. Johnson (2024). LORIS: A Lightweight Free-Climbing Robot for Extreme Terrain Exploration. *2024 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA)*, pp. 18480–18486. [doi:10.1109/icra57147.2024.10611653](https://doi.org/10.1109/icra57147.2024.10611653) · [open copy](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/app/uploads/2024/04/LORIS__A_Lightweight_Free_Climbing_Robot_for_Extreme_Terrain_Exploration.pdf)  
  Presents a lightweight free-climbing robot with fully passive microspine grippers and wrist joints, and an optimisation-based controller that distributes forces among the grippers to reduce the risk of unexpected detachment. The prototype climbs vertically on flat cinder-block walls and uneven rock in full Earth gravity.

- <a id="zi2024"></a>**Zi 2024.** Peijin Zi, Kun Xu, Jiawei Chen, Chang Wang, Tao Zhang, Yang Luo, Yaobin Tian, Li Wen and Xilun Ding (2024). Intelligent Rock-Climbing Robot Capable of Multimodal Locomotion and Hybrid Bioinspired Attachment. *Advanced Science* 11(39), article 2309058. [doi:10.1002/advs.202309058](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202309058) · [open copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11497113/)  
  Introduces MARCBot, a multimodal rock-climbing robot with spiny grippers inspired by beetles, arboreal birds and hoofed animals that serve for attachment when climbing and support when walking. A control strategy using dynamics and quadratic programming optimises the distribution of attachment wrenches.

- <a id="tanaka2025"></a>**Tanaka 2025.** Yusuke Tanaka, Yuki Shirai, Alexander Schperberg, Xuan Lin and Dennis Hong (2025). SCALER: Versatile Multilimbed Robot for Free-Climbing in Extreme Terrains. *IEEE Transactions on Robotics* 41, pp. 5189–5208. [doi:10.1109/tro.2025.3588446](https://doi.org/10.1109/tro.2025.3588446) · [open copy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04856)  
  Journal presentation of SCALER, a free-climbing multi-limbed robot designed for tightly coupled locomotion and grasping, with a torso mechanism, parallel-serial limbs and underactuated two-fingered GOAT grippers. Studies whole-body use of body and limbs to generate extra grasping force and faster gripper actuation for closed-loop dynamic climbing.

- <a id="uno2026"></a>**Uno 2026.** Kentaro Uno, Masazumi Imai, Kazuki Takada, Teruhiro Kataonami, Yudai Matsuura, Antonin Ringeval-Meusnier, Keita Nagaoka, Mikio Eguchi, Ryo Nishibe and Kazuya Yoshida (2026). LIMBERO: A Limbed Climbing Exploration Robot Toward Traveling on Rocky Cliffs. *2026 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA)*, pp. 21412–21419. [doi:10.1109/icra57385.2026.11696606](https://doi.org/10.1109/icra57385.2026.11696606) · [open copy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16531)  
  Presents LIMBERO, a quadrupedal climbing robot with spine-type grippers, including a gripper with coupled finger-closing and spine-hooking motions driven by one motor and an algorithm to visualise a geometry-based graspability index on rough terrain. The integrated robot is shown ascending steep rocky surfaces under Earth gravity.

## Spine grippers

- <a id="nagaoka2018"></a>**Nagaoka 2018.** Kenji Nagaoka, Hayato Minote, Kyohei Maruya, Yuki Shirai, Kazuya Yoshida, Takeshi Hakamada, Hirotaka Sawada and Takashi Kubota (2018). Passive Spine Gripper for Free-Climbing Robot in Extreme Terrain. *IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters* 3(3), pp. 1765–1770. [doi:10.1109/lra.2018.2794517](https://doi.org/10.1109/lra.2018.2794517)  
  Presents a passive spine gripper that holds rough rocky surfaces by passively and adaptively latching fine spines onto microscopic asperities, with its design, mechanism and a static gripping model verified on a prototype. A lightweight four-limbed robot carrying the grippers is tested climbing a rugged, steep slope.

- <a id="backus2020"></a>**Backus 2020.** Spencer B. Backus, Rina Onishi, Andrew Bocklund, Andrew Berg, Eric D. Contreras and Aaron Parness (2020). Design and testing of the JPL-Nautilus Gripper for deep-ocean geological sampling. *Journal of Field Robotics* 37(6), pp. 972–986. [doi:10.1002/rob.21934](https://doi.org/10.1002/rob.21934)  
  Presents the design and test results of the JPL-Nautilus Gripper, a 16-finger highly underactuated microspine gripper for the deep ocean that grasps objects and anchors to flat and curved rocky surfaces. Reports laboratory anchoring tests and deployment on the Hercules ROV during three deep-ocean dives.

- <a id="xu2020"></a>**Xu 2020.** Fengyu Xu, Fanchang Meng, Quansheng Jiang and Gaoliang Peng (2020). Grappling claws for a robot to climb rough wall surfaces: Mechanical design, grasping algorithm, and experiments. *Robotics and Autonomous Systems* 128, article 103501. [doi:10.1016/j.robot.2020.103501](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.robot.2020.103501)  
  Proposes an attachment method for robots on cliffs and dusty, vibrating rough walls using grappling-hook-like claws arranged in a cross shape, with simulated 3D wall profiles, conditions for stable grasping and a triangulation-based algorithm to judge which wall regions can be gripped. A prototype is tested on a vibrating wall and an outdoor wall.

- <a id="hu2021"></a>**Hu 2021.** Qiqiang Hu, Erbao Dong and Dong Sun (2021). Soft Gripper Design Based on the Integration of Flat Dry Adhesive, Soft Actuator, and Microspine. *IEEE Transactions on Robotics* 37(4), pp. 1065–1080. [doi:10.1109/tro.2020.3043981](https://doi.org/10.1109/tro.2020.3043981)  
  Reports a soft gripper that integrates a flat dry adhesive, a soft actuator and microspines to improve grasping on both smooth and rough surfaces, including a microspine-spring-shape-memory-alloy coil structure that retracts and protrudes the microspines and distributes load on rough surfaces.

- <a id="tanaka2021"></a>**Tanaka 2021.** Yusuke Tanaka, Yuki Shirai, Zachary Lacey, Xuan Lin, Jane Liu and Dennis Hong (2021). An Under-Actuated Whippletree Mechanism Gripper based on Multi-Objective Design Optimization with Auto-Tuned Weights. *2021 IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS)*, pp. 6139–6146. [doi:10.1109/iros51168.2021.9635872](https://doi.org/10.1109/iros51168.2021.9635872) · [open copy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00083)  
  Presents a rigid under-actuated gripper based on a whippletree mechanism that accommodates irregular and off-centre objects, together with a multi-objective parametric design optimisation for a one-wall climbing task that treats kinematics and grasping forces together. This is the paper that introduces the GOAT gripper later used on SCALER.

- <a id="panorel2025"></a>**Panorel & Nagaoka 2025.** Peter A. Panorel and Kenji Nagaoka (2025). Development of a self-locking spine gripper for power-efficient robotic climbing in extreme terrain. *Advanced Robotics* 39(21), pp. 1329–1345. [doi:10.1080/01691864.2025.2571561](https://doi.org/10.1080/01691864.2025.2571561)  
  Develops a Self-Locking Spine Gripper, a variant of an active spine gripper that maintains its gripping force without active actuation, to reduce the energy demand of climbing robots.

## Space and extreme terrain

- <a id="parness2011"></a>**Parness 2011.** Aaron Parness (2011). Anchoring foot mechanisms for sampling and mobility in microgravity. *2011 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA)*, pp. 6596–6599. [doi:10.1109/icra.2011.5980584](https://doi.org/10.1109/icra.2011.5980584)  
  Presents an omni-directional anchoring mechanism built on microspine toes, using an opposed octagonal scissor configuration with rows of toes on each lever arm splayed around a central housing. The anchor is developed for the LEMUR IIb robot and can also support a coring drill, for mobility and sampling in microgravity.

- <a id="parness2012"></a>**Parness 2012.** Aaron Parness, Matthew Frost, Nitish Thatte and Jonathan P. King (2012). Gravity-independent mobility and drilling on natural rock using microspines. *2012 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA)*, pp. 3437–3442. [doi:10.1109/icra.2012.6224933](https://doi.org/10.1109/icra.2012.6224933)  
  Presents a 250 mm diameter omni-directional anchor that uses a hierarchical array of microspines (claws with suspension flexures) to grip rock, demonstrated on vesicular basalt and a'a lava rock. ## Friction and limit surfaces

The limit surface applies to a wider class of friction laws than isotropic Coulomb friction, and both descriptions can be used to deduce the motion of sliding rigid bodies.

- <a id="labonte2015"></a>**Labonte & Federle 2015.** David Labonte and Walter Federle (2015). Scaling and biomechanics of surface attachment in climbing animals. *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B: Biological Sciences* 370(1661), article 20140027. [doi:10.1098/rstb.2014.0027](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2014.0027) · [open copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4275900/)  
  Discusses how contact geometry and mode of detachment influence the scaling of attachment forces with body size for claws and adhesive pads, and what allometric data reveal about attachment mechanisms. Suggests that switching between stress concentration and uniform load distribution via shear forces is a key mechanism for controlling adhesion during locomotion.

- <a id="song2016"></a>**Song 2016.** Yi Song, Zhendong Dai, Zhouyi Wang, Aihong Ji and Stanislav N. Gorb (2016). The synergy between the insect-inspired claws and adhesive pads increases the attachment ability on various rough surfaces. *Scientific Reports* 6(1), article 26219. [doi:10.1038/srep26219](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep26219) · open access  
  Tests an artificial attachment device that mimics insect claws and adhesive pads on stiff spheres of different sizes. Claw forces fall as sphere radius grows but can be very strong when the radius is smaller than or comparable to the claw radius, pads work on larger spheres, and the two together give stronger attachment than either alone.

- <a id="pattrick2018"></a>**Pattrick 2018.** Jonathan G. Pattrick, David Labonte and Walter Federle (2018). Scaling of claw sharpness: mechanical constraints reduce attachment performance in larger insects. *Journal of Experimental Biology* 221(24), article jeb188391. [doi:10.1242/jeb.188391](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.188391) · [open copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6811360/)  
  Examines the conflict between claw sharpness and fracture resistance by measuring how claw sharpness scales with body size in four insect species and measuring attachment forces on rough surfaces with a centrifuge. Larger animals were more likely to slip and attached with smaller forces relative to body weight.

- <a id="roderick2019"></a>**Roderick 2019.** William R. T. Roderick, Diana D. Chin, Mark R. Cutkosky and David Lentink (2019). Birds land reliably on complex surfaces by adapting their foot-surface interactions upon contact. *eLife* 8, article e46415. [doi:10.7554/elife.46415](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.46415) · [open copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6684272/)  
  Shows that Pacific parrotlets use stereotyped leg and wing dynamics when landing regardless of perch diameter and texture, while foot, toe and claw kinematics become surface-specific at touchdown. A dynamic grasping model indicates that the birds combine predictable toe-pad friction with probabilistic friction from claws that are dragged to find surface asperities.

## Reviews

- <a id="roderick2017"></a>**Roderick 2017.** William R. T. Roderick, Mark R. Cutkosky and David Lentink (2017). Touchdown to take-off: at the interface of flight and surface locomotion. *Interface Focus* 7(1), article 20160094. [doi:10.1098/rsfs.2016.0094](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsfs.2016.0094) · [open copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5206611/)  
  Review of bimodal aerial and surface locomotion in small robots and flying animals, covering the aerodynamics of surface approach and take-off, the contact dynamics of perching and attachment, and surface locomotion. Notes that the maximal size of perching animals and robots is limited by scaling laws for both adhesion and claw-based attachment.

- <a id="xu2022"></a>**Xu 2022.** Kun Xu, Peijin Zi and Xilun Ding (2022). Learning from biological attachment devices: applications of bioinspired reversible adhesive methods in robotics. *Frontiers of Mechanical Engineering* 17(3), article 43. [doi:10.1007/s11465-022-0699-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11465-022-0699-x) · open access  
  Systematic review of reversible biological adhesive methods (dry adhesion, wet adhesion, mechanical adhesion and sub-ambient pressure adhesion) and the bioinspired attachment devices derived from them, covering organ morphology, attachment models, device design and fabrication, design principles, and an overview of climbing robots that use such devices.

- <a id="akers2026"></a>**Akers 2026.** Michael Akers, Megan Michaud and Michael C.F. Bazzocchi (2026). Review, Design, and Comparative Analysis of Microspine Gripper Technologies for Asteroid Attachment. *2026 IEEE Aerospace Conference*, pp. 1–9. [doi:10.1109/aero66936.2026.11520149](https://doi.org/10.1109/aero66936.2026.11520149)  
  Reviews microspine gripper technology for attaching a lander to an asteroid, noting that most current designs fail under substantial compressive and torsional loads, and compares six alternative designs against six criteria with a multi-criteria decision process. The selected concept is a multi-phalanx configuration with radially symmetric leg sets.

Working documents and videos from the Stanford laboratory's project wikis, written or recorded at Stanford. They were not peer reviewed: treat their numbers as single observations. See [The BDML Z-Man wiki](../about/bdml-archive.md).

- <a id="bdml2007-prototypes"></a>**BDML 2007 (slides).** Stanford BDML, *Stanford prototyping, 8/8/07*, *Wire toes prototyping* and *Toes from nitinol array*, slides, August and September 2007. Unpublished; from the laboratory's Z-Man project wiki.  
  Photographs and short test results for the toes tried for human climbing in the summer of 2007: spring, spring-steel, embedded-wire and nitinol-wire toes, and the hand-held "ice axe".

- <a id="bdml2007-toe-design"></a>**Asbeck 2007 (slides).** Alan Asbeck, *Spiny toe design principles using WorkingModel*, slides, Stanford BDML, 26 July 2007. Unpublished; from the laboratory's Z-Man project wiki.  
  Rules of thumb for spiny toe linkages from two-dimensional rigid-body simulations: load along the wall before swinging to the working angle, prefer engaging to disengaging rotations of the spine, and use remote-centre compliance.

- <a id="bdml2008-discovery"></a>**BDML 2008 (internal review).** Stanford BDML, *Internal review of Discovery Channel human climbing project*, report, about 2008. Unpublished; from the laboratory's Z-Man project wiki.  
  The laboratory's own account of the climbing paddles it built for a television programme: design of the spine paddles, how they were loaded through the feet, what one paddle held on five surfaces, the climb, and lessons for later designs.

- <a id="bdml2009-hook-videos"></a>**BDML 2009 (videos).** Stanford BDML, two videos of ways to trim fishhooks for spine toes, early 2009. Unpublished; from the laboratory's Z-Man project wiki.  
  Two techniques for cutting the hooks used as spines, filmed at Stanford.

- <a id="bdml2009-opposed"></a>**Asbeck 2009 (slides).** Alan Asbeck, *Spiny gripper design*, slides and video, Stanford BDML, 4 March 2009. Unpublished; from the laboratory's Z-Man project wiki.  
  Design of an early opposed-spine gripper: two spiny feet on a balsa and carbon-fibre linkage, drawn together by a servo and string and retracted by a spring.

- <a id="esparza2009"></a>**Esparza 2009 (slides).** Noe Esparza, *Z-Man telecon*, slides, Stanford BDML, 9 April 2009. Unpublished; from the laboratory's Z-Man project wiki.  
  Includes a comparison of three ways to pick the failure point of each trial when mapping a limit surface on a force stage: largest adhesion, largest force magnitude, and largest power.
