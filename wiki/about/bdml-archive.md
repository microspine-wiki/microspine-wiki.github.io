# The BDML Z-Man wiki

## What it was

The Biomimetics and Dexterous Manipulation Laboratory (BDML) at Stanford ran a TWiki site through the 2000s, divided into "webs", one per project.

- The **Rise web**, named after the RiSE climbing-robot project, also held pages on the laboratory's other climbing robots, a list of publications with authors' copies of many of them, a page of videos and media coverage, and the notebooks of some students.
- The **Zman web** held the laboratory's notes on its part in the DARPA Z-Man programme, from 2007 until its front page was last edited in February 2011. That front page calls it "primarily intended as a shared electronic notebook for members of the research team", adding that "visitors are welcome to peruse the public pages". It covered both halves of the programme, gecko-like dry adhesives for smooth surfaces and microspines for rough ones, together with the test apparatus, surface measurements and models that served both.

The laboratory later moved to a new wiki, and a banner on every old page points there.

## What state it is in

The old pages are still served at `bdml.stanford.edu/twiki/`, as a frozen copy:

- nothing can be edited;
- many links point to pages that were never written, marked with a question mark;
- many videos need the QuickTime or DivX codecs, which current browsers do not support.

If a link to the old wiki in a paper no longer works, try the Internet Archive's [Wayback Machine](https://web.archive.org/), which holds captures of some of its pages.

Text from these documents is summarised in the wiki's own words. Photographs are resized and videos re-encoded for the web; the long video of the climb is also sped up. Nothing else is changed. Every item is listed on [Credits and licences](credits.md#from-the-laboratorys-project-wikis), and its rights remain with its authors.

Working documents were not peer reviewed. They contain first impressions, numbers from single trials and conclusions their authors later revised. Pages that use them say so, and the bibliography lists them in a section of their own ([Unpublished laboratory documents](../reference/bibliography.md#unpublished-laboratory-documents)). Where a published account exists, it takes precedence.

## What is not here

- **Material from other organisations in the programme.** Anything written or produced by another organisation taking part in Z-Man, or carrying its proprietary markings, is not reproduced, quoted or linked.
- **Dry adhesives.** Much of the archive concerns gecko-like adhesives for smooth surfaces. That work is outside this wiki's scope.
- **Logins, meeting details and personal pages.**
- **Drafts and raw data where a published version exists.** The published paper or thesis is cited instead.

## If you were there

If you took part in this work, or hold rights in material from it, you can make this record better.

- **Write it down.** A paragraph on what you learned, under your own name, is a citable source. See [Contributing](contributing.md).
- **Release what you can.** A report, data set or video deposited in a university repository or a public archive, with a licence, can be cited and summarised here.
- **Correct the record.** If something on this wiki is wrong or unfair, or if you want an item from the archive taken down, say so.

The record is still thin on the questions a builder of large systems would ask: how performance varied from wall to wall, and what was tried and abandoned after 2009. Those gaps are listed in [Open questions](../reference/open-questions.md#history).

Contributors who hold unreleased material and are waiting for permission can keep their notes alongside the wiki without publishing them. See [material that is not cleared](contributing.md#material-that-is-not-cleared).
